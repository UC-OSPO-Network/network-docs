# UCLA Open Source Resource Cheat Sheet

Working campus inventory for the UC OSPO Network  
Last verified: July 23, 2026

> **Status key:** **Confirmed** means an authoritative UCLA page supports the entry. **Needs campus confirmation** means the resource is plausible or decentralized, but the answer should be checked with the responsible UCLA unit before publication.

# Facet 1: Discoverability

## Scholarly preservation

### Does UCLA have premium subscriptions to scholarly data repositories? Where can people get assistance?

**Confirmed.** UCLA researchers have access to:

- [UCLA Dataverse](https://dataverse.ucla.edu/), an institutional repository for publishing and preserving research data.
- [Protocols.io](https://www.library.ucla.edu/help/services-resources/open-science-services/).
- [Dryad](https://datadryad.org/), available across the UC system.
- Other supported preservation and dissemination resources include [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/), Vivli, and discipline-appropriate repositories.

The best starting point is the UCLA Library’s [Open Science Services](https://www.library.ucla.edu/help/services-resources/open-science-services/). UCLA Library staff can help with repository selection, data publication, data management, persistent identifiers, and open-science workflows.

### Does UCLA have a software catalog where people can list or promote open source projects?

**Needs campus confirmation.** UCLA does not appear to have a comprehensive, campus-wide public catalog of open source research software. The UC OSPO Network’s software-discovery work may provide a future pathway. UCLA project repositories are currently distributed across individual GitHub organizations, labs, centers, and departmental sites.

### Are there other groups, educational resources, or services for scholarly software management, preservation, or archival?

**Confirmed.**

- The UCLA Library [Data Science Center](https://www.library.ucla.edu/about/programs/data-science-center/) supports reproducible research, open science, research data, software-based methods, and consultations.
- The [Office of Advanced Research Computing (OARC)](https://oarc.ucla.edu/) provides research computing, software-development, data, cloud, and high-performance-computing expertise.
- [OARC Research Software Development](https://oarc.ucla.edu/get-help/areas-expertise/research-software-development) supports simulation, containerization, full-stack development, APIs, cloud deployment, and Hoffman2-based software.
- [UCLA Carpentries](https://ucla-data-archive.github.io/ucla-carpentries/) provides foundational training in reproducible computing and data skills.
- UCLA Library [Open Science Services](https://www.library.ucla.edu/help/services-resources/open-science-services/) supports tools including GitHub-related workflows, OSF, Zenodo, ORCID, DMPTool, and eScholarship.

### Are there resources or workshops to help researchers articulate software impact for tenure and promotion?

**Needs campus confirmation.** No centralized UCLA program specifically focused on software citation, software metrics, or documenting software impact in academic review was identified. The Library, OARC, and UCLA OSPO participants have relevant expertise, but this appears to be a service and workshop gap.

## Licensing

### Who is UCLA’s technology transfer office?

**Confirmed.** UCLA’s technology transfer office is the [Technology Development Group (TDG)](https://tdg.ucla.edu/).

### Does TDG have resources of interest to open source software or hardware creators?

**Confirmed.**

- TDG explains [open source and other software-sharing options](https://tdg.ucla.edu/about/faq/open-source-other-software-sharing-options).
- TDG provides [source-available licenses for sharing software code for noncommercial use](https://tdg.ucla.edu/about/faq/open-source-other-software-sharing-options/licenses-sharing-software-code-non).
- UCLA creators should consult TDG before releasing software when the work may constitute a university invention, has commercialization potential, includes third-party obligations, or was developed under sponsored research.

**Needs campus confirmation:** whether TDG maintains additional ready-to-sign agreements relevant to software or hardware that are not linked from its public software-sharing pages.

## Documentation

### Does UCLA have resources to help researchers write software documentation?

**Partly confirmed.** UCLA does not appear to have one central documentation clinic or recurring documentation sprint. Relevant support includes:

- UCLA Library Data Science Center and UCLA Carpentries consultations and workshops on reproducible research, Git, GitHub, and project workflows.
- OARC research-software consultations for development and deployment.
- [UCLA Writing Programs](https://wp.ucla.edu/) and discipline-based writing support, although these are not specifically software-documentation services.

This is a good candidate for a UCLA OSPO workshop or documentation sprint.

# Facet 2: Funding

### Who is UCLA’s Sponsored Projects Office?

**Confirmed.** The [Office of Contract and Grant Administration (OCGA)](https://ocga.research.ucla.edu/) reviews, approves, and submits proposals to government and nonprofit sponsors and supports award administration.

### Does UCLA have a central office for grant-writing assistance?

**Confirmed, with scope caveats.** The [Research Enhancement Office (REO)](https://www3.research.ucla.edu/reo) provides research development, funding information, limited-submission coordination, training, and a Research Concierge function. Its [workshops, events, and trainings](https://www3.research.ucla.edu/reo/events) include proposal-writing programs. Schools and departments may also provide local research-development staff.

### What tool does UCLA recommend for finding grant opportunities, and where can people get help?

**Confirmed.** UCLA provides institution-wide access to the [SPIN funding opportunity database](https://www3.research.ucla.edu/reo/fundopps). It includes customizable searches and alerts and is available to UCLA faculty, staff, and enrolled students through institutional sign-in. REO also maintains newsletters and lists of federal, state, foundation, corporate, UC, and internal opportunities.

### Where can researchers recruit student interns or find student-research matching programs?

**Confirmed.**

- The [Undergraduate Research Portal](https://sciences.ugresearch.ucla.edu/courses/srp/) allows faculty to advertise research opportunities for academic credit.
- The [Undergraduate Research Center–Sciences](https://sciences.ugresearch.ucla.edu/) supports research in life and physical sciences, engineering, and mathematics and explains [how to recruit undergraduates](https://sciences.ugresearch.ucla.edu/resources/how-to-recruit-undergraduates/).
- The [Undergraduate Research Center–Humanities, Arts, and Social Sciences](https://hass.ugresearch.ucla.edu/) serves the remaining disciplines.
- Departments, work-study hiring, Data Science Union, student organizations, and capstone programs may offer additional project-based recruitment channels.

### Who is UCLA’s Office of Development?

**Confirmed.** [UCLA Development](https://www.uclafoundation.org/about/development) and the [UCLA Foundation](https://www.uclafoundation.org/) support campus fundraising and philanthropic relationships. School and unit development teams usually handle project-specific engagement.

### Are there development programs of interest to open source creators, such as crowdfunding or gift sponsorship?

**Needs campus confirmation.** UCLA previously operated UCLA Spark as a crowdfunding platform, but its current availability and eligibility should be confirmed before recommending it. Creators should first consult their school or unit development office about gifts, donor restrictions, and whether a software or hardware project is suitable for philanthropic support.

### Does UCLA have a Corporate Relations Office?

**Confirmed, but decentralized.** UCLA has corporate and foundation relations functions in [UCLA External Affairs](https://www.externalaffairs.ucla.edu/) and school-level offices. TDG also serves as a campus gateway for industry engagement involving research, technology, and commercialization.

### Does UCLA have industry consortia?

**Confirmed, but no complete central directory was identified.** Examples include membership and affiliate programs associated with UCLA Engineering, the California NanoSystems Institute, Institute of the Environment and Sustainability, UCLA Health, and discipline-specific research centers. TDG maintains [industry research funding opportunities](https://tdg.ucla.edu/ucla-researchers/industry-research-funding-opportunities).

**Needs campus confirmation:** a comprehensive campus list and which programs accept software-centered projects.

### Does UCLA have legal clinics relevant to open source creators?

**Partly confirmed.** UCLA School of Law operates experiential clinics, but no standing public clinic dedicated specifically to open source licensing was identified. The [Documentary Film Legal Clinic](https://law.ucla.edu/academics/clinical-education/clinics/documentary-film-legal-clinic) and technology- or entrepreneurship-related programs may address adjacent intellectual-property issues. University-owned software questions should begin with TDG and, where appropriate, UCLA counsel rather than a student clinic.

### What incubator or accelerator programs are available?

**Confirmed, non-exhaustive.**

- [UCLA Anderson Venture Accelerator](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/about/centers/price-center-for-entrepreneurship-and-innovation/anderson-venture-accelerator): Activate, Accelerate, founder programming, mentoring, and showcase opportunities.
- [Magnify at CNSI](https://www.cnsi.ucla.edu/magnify/): incubator support for technology and life-science ventures.
- [UCLA Biodesign](https://biodesign.ucla.edu/): health-technology innovation and venture development.
- [UCLA Technology Development Group](https://tdg.ucla.edu/): commercialization guidance, startup resources, and industry connections.
- School- and student-based programs, including entrepreneurship programs at Anderson and Engineering.

Because eligibility varies among students, faculty, staff, alumni, and outside founders, creators should check each program directly.

### Does UCLA have an entrepreneur-in-residence program?

**Needs campus confirmation.** UCLA has mentors, advisors, executives, and founders-in-residence distributed across TDG, Anderson, CNSI/Magnify, Engineering, and health-innovation programs, but no single central campus-wide entrepreneur-in-residence program was confirmed.

### Does UCLA have internal seed grants that may fund software development?

**Confirmed.**

- REO maintains a searchable [Internal Funding Opportunities database](https://www3.research.ucla.edu/reo/internalfunding/database).
- The [UCLA Innovation Fund](https://tdg.ucla.edu/ucla-researchers/ucla-innovation-fund) supports development of promising technologies, with calls and eligibility changing by cycle.
- Academic Senate, school, institute, DataX, CTSI, and transdisciplinary programs periodically offer seed funding.

Software-development costs may be eligible when they are integral to the proposed research or innovation, but each solicitation should be checked.

### Are there other funding-related resources open source creators should know about?

**Confirmed.**

- [TDG industry funding opportunities](https://tdg.ucla.edu/ucla-researchers/industry-research-funding-opportunities).
- REO’s [funding opportunity resources and SPIN](https://www3.research.ucla.edu/reo/fundopps).
- UCLA Anderson’s founder programs and competitions.
- OARC consultations can help researchers scope computational infrastructure and software-development needs for grant budgets.

# Facet 3: Community

### Where can maintainers get guidance on governance or conflict resolution?

**Confirmed.**

- [UCLA Office of Ombuds Services](https://ombuds.ucla.edu/) offers confidential, neutral, informal assistance with workplace and academic conflicts.
- [UCLA Equity, Diversity and Inclusion](https://equity.ucla.edu/) provides policy, education, and campus resources.
- The [Office of Academic Excellence and Engagement](https://equity.ucla.edu/) and local human-resources or academic-personnel offices may be relevant depending on the participants and issue.
- For project governance rather than interpersonal disputes, UCLA Library, OARC, and UC OSPO Network participants can advise on contributor guidelines, codes of conduct, and sustainable community practices.

### Does UCLA subscribe to self-guided learning platforms?

**Confirmed.** UCLA employees have access to [LinkedIn Learning](https://www.linkedin.com/learning/) through UC/UCLA professional-development systems. Availability for students and affiliates may differ. UCLA also provides institutionally licensed learning resources through individual schools, the Library, and IT units.

# Facet 4: Product design

### Does UCLA have research computing or HPC consulting for optimization, containerization, and scaling?

**Confirmed.** [OARC](https://oarc.ucla.edu/) operates and supports the Hoffman2 shared cluster and provides consulting in high-performance computing, cloud computing, research software, data science, visualization, and related areas. Its [Research Software Development](https://oarc.ucla.edu/get-help/areas-expertise/research-software-development) service explicitly includes software containerization, application and API development, simulation, cloud deployment, and Hoffman2 optimization.

The Library Data Science Center complements OARC with consultations and education in reproducible research, research data, open science, and software-based methods.

### Does UCLA have an accessibility office that supports software and documentation?

**Confirmed.** UCLA’s [Disabilities and Computing Program](https://dcp.ucla.edu/) provides digital-accessibility expertise, training, testing guidance, and consultation. Its [UCLA Web Accessibility Initiative](https://dcp.ucla.edu/uwai) is the primary starting point for web and digital accessibility standards. Project teams should involve accessibility expertise early and align with UC policy and WCAG requirements.

### What UCLA resources exist for AI usage?

**Partly confirmed; rapidly changing.**

- UCLA’s central IT and information-security guidance should be consulted before entering university, research, student, health, or restricted data into an AI service.
- UCLA Google Workspace provides access to Google tools; availability and contractual data protections for Gemini and NotebookLM depend on the user’s account and current campus agreement.
- UCLA Library provides instruction and guidance on generative AI, information literacy, data, copyright, and research use.
- [OARC](https://oarc.ucla.edu/) and UCLA DataX support research computing, data science, machine learning, and AI-related events and expertise.
- UCLA Health has separate governance and approved-tool requirements for clinical and protected health information.

**Needs campus confirmation:** the current list of centrally licensed generative-AI tools, eligible populations, data-classification limits, and whether UCLA has a general-purpose protected institutional AI environment comparable to UCSB’s AI Commons.

# Miscellaneous

### Other UCLA resources

- The UCLA Library Data Science Center provides research consultations, workshops, DataSquad support, UCLA Dataverse administration, open-science services, and spatial/3D/data-methods expertise.
- UCLA is a member of the UC OSPO Network, which can connect creators to open source licensing, education, project-health, community, and sustainability expertise across participating UC campuses.
- OARC and the Library offer complementary entry points: OARC for advanced computing and software engineering, and the Library for data stewardship, reproducibility, open science, methods, and preservation.
