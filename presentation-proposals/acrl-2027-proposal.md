# ACRL 2027 Proposal

## Title

No Library is an Island: Cross-Institutional Networks as a Strategy for Sustaining Specialized Research Support

## Abstract

Research support has outpaced what most libraries can realistically staff. Data management, open science, research software, reproducibility. Each area now requires genuine depth that is expensive to build and harder to keep. The usual answer is to hire specialists campus by campus. The result is familiar: thin coverage, high turnover, expertise that gets reinvented every time someone leaves.

There is a structural alternative. Cross-institutional networks can distribute the labor and cost of building specialized services in ways that make them sustainable at a scale no single institution could achieve alone.

This session draws on three UC programs I've been part of building. UC Carpentries now counts 60+ certified instructors across ten campuses. The UC OSPO Network, funded by the Alfred P. Sloan Foundation, coordinates open source program development across six campuses. An IMLS-funded Open Science curriculum project produced openly licensed lessons now in use across library and research communities. These programs look different from each other. But they share structure: defined scope, shared governance, explicit expectations about who does what.

A recent Ithaka S+R issue brief on system-level open source offices found that library coordination networks are emerging as a governance model precisely because they do not require centralized authority or uniform capacity. That is worth generalizing.

This session is not a case for replicating what UC built. It offers something more portable: a diagnostic for identifying which services in your library are candidates for federation, and what governance questions to work through before approaching a potential partner. Attendees leave with a framework for knowing where cross-institutional collaboration makes sense, and where it does not.
